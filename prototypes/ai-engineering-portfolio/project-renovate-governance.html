Renovate governance ladder

A trust ladder for dependency automation — supervised patches at the bottom, earned autonomy higher up — with stop conditions you can audit.

GovernanceAutomationOps

On this page

Problem Role System overview Key decisions Outcomes Evidence Related

Problem

Teams either fear Renovate and ignore PRs, or auto-merge too aggressively and learn about breakages in production. Binary automation settings do not match how trust actually accumulates.

Role

Designed the ladder model, policy matrix, and evidence requirements for promoting a package family to the next autonomy tier.

System overview

Tiers map update types and blast radius to required checks: human review, canary green, changelog classification, and rollback readiness. Promotion and demotion are explicit events in an audit log.

System overview · Renovate governance ladder

Key engineering decisions

Decision 01
Ladder not toggle

Autonomy is earned per package family, not global.

Decision 02
Stop conditions

Failed canary, unexpected major bump, or missing release notes halt the climb.

Decision 03
Explainability

Every merged update cites the tier rule that allowed it.

Decision 04
Human override

On-call can freeze a family without disabling Renovate entirely.

Outcomes

  • Dependency noise drops because low-risk patches move faster under clear rules.
  • High-risk upgrades stay supervised with a documented reason.
  • Governance becomes a reusable pattern for other agentic ops (not only Renovate).

No invented KPIs — outcomes are qualitative until you supply measured results.
